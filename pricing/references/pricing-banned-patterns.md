# Pricing patterns the skill refuses to produce

The skill pushes back with the JMC alternative when the operator asks
for any of these. Many of them are common — that's why they're banned;
they fail predictably.

---

## Banned: Cost-plus pricing

> "We figured out our cost was $20/user, doubled it, and called it
> $40/user."

**Why it fails:** Cost has nothing to do with what the buyer is
willing to pay. Cost-plus prices for the seller's spreadsheet, not
the buyer's reference frame.

**JMC alternative:** Anchor on inertia value or replacement cost.
Cost is the floor (you can't price below it sustainably), not the
reference.

---

## Banned: Competitor-anchored pricing

> "Their plan is $299, ours is $279."

**Why it fails:** Competitor cost is the weakest reference frame
in the stack (rank 4 of 4). Whoever leads with "we're cheaper" loses
the value conversation. The buyer will keep shopping.

**JMC alternative:** Anchor on inertia ("you're solving this today
with $X of internal time + $Y of patchwork tools") or opportunity
cost ("every week you delay = $Z of pipeline you don't close").

---

## Banned: Open-ended money-back guarantees

> "Money-back guarantee if you're not satisfied."

**Why it fails:** Buyers don't believe it ("there's always fine
print"). Sellers can't enforce it ("what does 'satisfied' mean").
Loss aversion makes the buyer protective — claiming the refund feels
risky.

**JMC alternative:** Outcome-tied guarantees with bounded liability
and explicit qualification gates. "If we don't deliver X by date Y,
you pay only Z." Specific, falsifiable, enforceable.

---

## Banned: "Contact us for pricing"

> Below the $30K ACV threshold without a real strategic-customer motion.

**Why it fails:** Adds friction without lift. Buyer drops off before
sales gets the lead. Signals "expensive" without anchoring the value.
Below mid-market, the buyer needs a number to feel safe to evaluate.

**JMC alternative:** Show a price. If you have one Enterprise tier
above ~$30K ACV, anchor it with a "starts at" number — never an
unbounded "contact us."

---

## Banned: Two-tier pricing without a decoy

> "We have a Basic plan and a Pro plan."

**Why it fails:** Without a third tier, there's no contrast set.
Buyers have nothing to compare against. The decision becomes binary
(buy / don't buy) when it should be a question of WHICH tier.

**JMC alternative:** Three tiers with an intentional decoy.
Asymmetric dominance — the decoy makes the target obviously better.

---

## Banned: Four-or-more-tier pricing

> "We have Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, and Enterprise."

**Why it fails:** Decision paralysis. Buyer spends 20 minutes
comparing tiers instead of buying. Feature differentiation gets
muddled. The fourth tier always cannibalizes the third.

**JMC alternative:** Three tiers, max. If you need a fourth, you
probably need a separate product line — not another tier.

---

## Banned: Per-seat pricing when the value is not per-seat

> "We charge $29/user/month for a tool used twice a week."

**Why it fails:** Buyer has incentive to share logins. Volume-vs-
revenue tension. The metric doesn't grow with their success — it
grows with their HR org.

**JMC alternative:** Per-active-record / per-outcome / per-event.
The metric scales with their growth, not their headcount policy.

---

## Banned: Discounting without policy

> "We give 20% off if they say it's expensive."

**Why it fails:** Discounts trained into the sales motion become
the new ceiling. Buyers learn that asking for 20% off works, so
everyone asks. Pocket price drops by the average ask, not the
average grant.

**JMC alternative:** Documented discount policy with approval
thresholds. Sales-rep discretion bounded (e.g., up to 10% without
approval). Discounts tied to commercial concessions (longer term,
prepay, reference) — never just to close.

---

## Banned: Annual prepay with no real price difference

> "$99/month or $1,000/year (~16% off)."

**Why it fails:** Buyer does the math. Sees $188 off. Wonders why
you're not offering the monthly rate. Trust erodes.

**JMC alternative:** Either make the annual prepay a meaningful
discount (≥25%) tied to commercial value (locking churn risk, cash
flow), or remove the option. Halfway pricing reads as either
incompetence or manipulation.

---

## Banned: Raising prices without grandfathering

> "Everyone's price goes up 25% next quarter."

**Why it fails:** Mass churn event. Trust hit you can't recover
from. Existing customers feel betrayed; their CFO finds the line
item. Sales spends two quarters in retention conversations.

**JMC alternative:** Grandfather existing customers indefinitely or
with a long ramp (12-24 months). Raise prices on NEW logos only.
Communicate the new price as anchored on increased value (and
deliver it).

---

## Banned: "Save 50% with this discount code"

> Permanent promo banner on the pricing page.

**Why it fails:** Becomes the new list price. Trains the buyer that
the headline number isn't real. Triggers ad-policy issues with paid
acquisition. Signals "no one buys at list."

**JMC alternative:** If 50% off is the real price, lower the list
price. If it's a real campaign, make it bounded (end date, finite
seats) and remove the banner after.

---

## Banned: Pricing copy that anchors on features

> "Get unlimited workflows, 10GB storage, priority support."

**Why it fails:** Feature lists don't drive WTP. The buyer can't
translate features into "is this worth $X for me." Different buyers
weight different features. The page reads like a spec sheet, not a
purchase decision aid.

**JMC alternative:** Anchor on outcomes per PSP. "For founders with
~$3M ARR and a 4-rep sales team: ship a working pricing page in 30
days, with quarterly review built in." Buyer self-selects.

---

## Banned: One-size-fits-all pricing across PSPs

> "Same $299/mo plan whether you're a 5-person team or a 500-person team."

**Why it fails:** Leaves money on the table at the high end + prices
out the low end. WTP varies dramatically by PSP. A single price
serves one PSP well and the rest poorly.

**JMC alternative:** Tiered pricing with PSP-aware feature
differentiation. The decoy serves the low-WTP PSP (or routes them
to self-serve). The target serves the dominant PSP. The anchor
serves the high-WTP PSP.

---

## Banned: "We'll figure pricing out later"

> "Let's launch with no price and figure it out from customer
> feedback."

**Why it fails:** Free is a different product than paid. Free users
give different signals than paying users. You can't "raise" from $0
to $X without churning every free user. Free-to-paid is its own
discipline; defaulting to it is not.

**JMC alternative:** Charge from day one, even if you're guessing.
Set a price that's "uncomfortably high" — closer to your WTP ceiling
than floor. Adjust based on close-rate data. The discipline is
having a number to test against.

---

## Banned: Decoy pricing without asymmetric dominance

> "Our cheapest tier has half the features but a different value
> metric (per-API-call instead of per-seat)."

**Why it fails:** A decoy needs to be obviously worse on the SAME
metric. Different metrics create a "comparing apples to oranges"
read — the buyer can't quickly grok that Tier 2 is better.

**JMC alternative:** Decoy has the same value metric (per-seat /
per-call / etc.) but strictly less of it. Or strictly fewer features
in the same feature set. Buyer recognizes the asymmetry in <5 seconds.

---

## Banned: Hidden fees / surprise charges

> "$99/month + $500 onboarding fee (disclosed at checkout) + $50
> per integration."

**Why it fails:** Trust erosion at the worst possible moment. Buyer
abandons cart. Churn-on-discovery if they don't. The actual cost
should match the displayed cost within ~5%.

**JMC alternative:** Itemize every cost on the pricing page.
Onboarding, integrations, support tiers — every line item visible
before checkout. "Configured price" model: buyer picks options,
total updates live, no surprises.

---

## Banned: Auto-escalating prices without renewal notice

> "Year 2 price is 12% higher; quietly applied at renewal."

**Why it fails:** Discovery → churn → public review → ad-policy /
G2-rating consequences. The CFO finds the increase eventually. Even
"reasonable" auto-escalation reads as bad faith if it's not flagged
60+ days before renewal.

**JMC alternative:** Renewal escalation is fine — communicate it 90
days before the renewal date, in writing, with the reason (CPI / new
features / market). Give the buyer space to push back or budget.

---

## When the operator pushes back

If the operator insists on a banned pattern, the skill:

1. Pushes back with the rationale (cites which JMC principle is
   violated)
2. Offers the alternative
3. If the operator still insists, generates the asked-for pattern
   with a flagged warning ("⚠ This pattern fails predictably in the
   JMC framework. See `pricing-banned-patterns.md` rule N.")

The skill never silently produces a banned pattern.
